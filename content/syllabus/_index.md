### Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, students will be able to:

- Explain how biotic and abiotic processes combine to drive the dynamics of plant populations 
(IDEA objective 1: Gaining a basic understanding of the subject). 

- Build and analyze mathematical models that represent the influence of these biotic and abiotic processes on plant population dynamics 
(IDEA objective 13: Learning appropriate methods for collecting, analyzing, and interpreting numerical information). 

- Collaborate effectively on group projects (IDEA objective 5: Acquiring skills in working with others as a member of a team).

### General Course Structure and Expectations

In this class, we link each of the conceptual topics listed below with its implementation in a mathematical model. 
For each topic, we will discuss readings and then play with the corresponding model. 
The models will be increasingly elaborate variations on one basic population model, not stand-alone, separate models. 
Between the reading assignments and the computer labs (group assignments), you should learn the concepts and gain 
quantitative skills in modeling and coding. No previous coding or modeling experience is required.

There is also a final project for this course. We will provide time in class towards the end of the course to work on the projects.

### Grading

25% of your grade will be based on class participation, especially discussion of assigned readings. 

50% will be based on problem sets (computer labs). Problem sets will be submitted on the course Canvas page.

25% will be based on a final project.

Grades at the end of the course will be assigned as: A (93 to 100% of total possible points); A- (90 to 92.9%); B+ (87 to 89.9%); B (83 to 86.9%); B- (80 to 82.9%); C+ (77 to 79.9%); C (73 to 76.9%); C- (70 to 72.9%); D (60 to 69.9%); F (<59.9%)

### Late policy

Problem sets submitted after the deadline will receive a 10% penalty. Each subsequent 24 hr delay will result in an 
additional 10% penalty. 

We will drop your two lowest problem set scores before calculating your semester average score. This means you can miss
two of the problem sets (group projects) entirely. Please do not ask for permission to miss a class or a problem set until
you have used up these two drops.

### AI philosophy

You can use AI however you like. But remember that learning requires effort. The more you struggle with a problem 
before asking AI for an answer, or the more time you spend on a reading assignment, the more you will learn and retain 
(up to a point: being stuck for 30 minutes can be educational, but being stuck for days is a waste of time). 
If you do use AI for help with a problem set, please let us know when and how you used it. 
We may be able to help you use it more effectively to promote, not replace, learning.
